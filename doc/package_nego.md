# Package_nego

An actionable playbook for negotiating a compensation offer. Company-neutral.

## 1) Mindset

- **Negotiating is expected.** An offer is the start of a conversation, not a
  final verdict. Recruiters do this daily; declining to negotiate leaves money
  on the table.
- **They already want you.** The offer means the expensive part (deciding to
  hire) is done. Rescinding an offer over a polite negotiation is extremely rare.
- **Be collaborative, not adversarial.** You and the recruiter both want you to
  say yes. Frame everything as "help me get to yes."
- **Stay positive and grateful** throughout — enthusiasm plus a firm ask is the
  winning tone.

---

## 2) Compensation Components

Total comp is more than base salary. Know every lever:

| Component | Notes / negotiable? |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Base salary** | Recurring, lowest risk. Often the least flexible at big firms, most flexible at smaller ones. |
| **Annual bonus** | % of base, tied to performance/company. Ask for target % and historical payout. |
| **Equity** | RSUs (public) or options (private). Ask: how many units, current value, **vesting schedule**, cliff. |
| **Sign-on bonus** | One-time cash. Easiest lever to move; often has a clawback if you leave early. |
| **Relocation** | Lump sum or reimbursed. |
| **Other** | 401k match, PTO, remote flexibility, title/level, start date, learning budget. |

**Level matters most.** The band (level/title) sets the range for everything
else — negotiating the level up can dwarf any single component.

**Equity fluency**
- Vesting: e.g. 4 years with a 1-year cliff (nothing vests until month 12).
- Public RSUs ≈ cash on a schedule; private options are a bet — ask about
  strike price, preferred vs common, latest valuation, dilution.
- Annualize equity to compare offers: total grant / vesting years.

## 3) Before You Negotiate

1. **Know your number** and your **walk-away** floor.
2. **Research market rates** for the role, level, and location (levels/comp
   aggregator sites, peers, recruiters).
3. **Have (or create) leverage** — competing offers, a current job, a strong
   pipeline. Even a strong interview loop is leverage.
4. **Get the offer in writing** with the full breakdown before responding.

---

## 4) Handling "What are your salary expectations?"

The person who names a number first is often anchored by it. **Deflect early,
politely.**

- "I'm more focused on finding the right fit — I'm confident we can agree on a
  fair number if it's the right role. What's the budgeted range for this level?"
- "I'd rather understand the full picture first. What range do you have in mind?"
- If pressed and you must answer, give a **researched range** anchored at the top
  of market, and tie it to data: "Based on market rates for this level, I'd
  expect total comp in the range of X–Y."

Never lie about existing offers or current salary — but you're not obligated to
disclose your current salary.

---

## 5) Making the Ask

- **Anchor high but defensible.** Ask for more than your target so there's room
  to meet in the middle.
- **Negotiate total comp, not just base.** Give them multiple levers to satisfy
  you (base, sign-on, equity) — flexibility helps them say yes.
- **Justify with value and market data**, not personal need ("competing offers at
  X" / "market for this level is Y"), never "I need it to pay rent."
- **Ask, then go quiet.** State the ask and stop talking; let them respond.
- **Get every change in writing** before accepting.

Sample script:
> "Thank you — I'm really excited about this team. The base of $X is a bit below
> what I was targeting given the market for this level and my competing
> conversations. If we can get the base to $Y and increase the sign-on to $Z,
> I'd be ready to sign."

---

## 6) Competing Offers & Timing

- **Competing offers are your strongest leverage.** Mention them honestly; you
  don't need exact figures to signal you have options.
- **Align timelines.** If one offer's deadline is too early, ask for an extension
  ("I'm excited but want to finish my other processes to make a confident
  decision — can we extend to [date]?"). Reasonable extensions are normal.
- **Let firms know you're deciding between offers** — it motivates their best number.
- Don't bluff with fake offers; it can backfire and it's unethical.

---

## 7) Common Mistakes

- Accepting on the spot out of excitement or fear.
- Giving a hard number too early with no research.
- Negotiating only base and ignoring equity/sign-on/level.
- Justifying the ask with personal need instead of market value.
- Being adversarial, issuing ultimatums, or bluffing offers you don't have.
- Not getting the final terms in writing.
- Forgetting non-cash levers (level, remote work, start date, PTO).
- Under-valuing the **level/title** — the single biggest long-term lever.

---

## 8) Quick Checklist

- [ ] Full written offer with every component
- [ ] Researched market range for role + level + location
- [ ] Target number and walk-away floor set
- [ ] Leverage identified (competing offers / current role)
- [ ] Deflected the "expectations" question or gave a researched range
- [ ] Anchored high, negotiated total comp, justified with data
- [ ] Aligned deadlines across offers
- [ ] Confirmed final terms in writing before accepting
